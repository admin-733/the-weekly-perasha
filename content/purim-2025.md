The Gemara in Megillah (13b) teaches that Haman was an expert in Lashon Hara. Step by step, he carefully crafted his arguments against the Jewish people, convincing King Achashverosh to decree their destruction. His hateful words carried immense power — power that nearly led to catastrophe.

But if words can be used for destruction, they can also be used for building, uplifting, and bringing light into the world. The Zohar teaches that even when speaking for good purposes, we must be careful with our words. This reminds us just how significant our speech is.

We all know the saying: "With great power comes great responsibility." It's not always easy to say the right thing, but little by little, with effort, we can become masters — not like Haman, who used his words for evil, but masters of speaking positively about others.

Here are some simple ways to elevate your speech:

- Give a genuine compliment — lift someone's spirits.
- Express gratitude — a simple "thank you" goes a long way.
- Defend others — stand up when you hear unfair criticism.
- Refrain from negativity — if it's not helpful, hold back.
- Spread words of Torah and encouragement — inspire those around you.

This Purim, let's use our words to build, not destroy, to unite, not divide. In doing so, may we merit Hashem's continued protection and blessings — just as the Jewish people were saved in the days of Mordechai and Esther.

Hag Purim Sameah!
