Perashat Tesave is famously the only perasha in the Torah, from Moshe's birth until his death, where his name does not appear. Next week in Ki Tissa, when G-d threatens to destroy the Jewish People after the sin of the Golden Calf, Moshe intercedes and declares, "And now, if You will bear their sin, but if not, erase me from Your book." (Shemot 32:32). The Ba'al HaTurim (Shemot 27:20) notes that even Moshe's conditional "erase me" declaration is fulfilled in some measure, and so his name is missing from Tesave.

Why Tesave specifically? The perasha opens with the words "And you shall command" (Shemot 27:20). It is clear that the "you" is referring to Moshe as the continuation of the laws that began last week. The Lubavitcher Rebbe ("Ve'atah Tesave" Purim Katan 5741 / 1981) takes this further, explaining that by not stating "Moshe," the Torah is speaking to something deeper than his name. It is addressing his very essence, the core of who Moshe is. His name may be absent, but that essence fills every line of the perasha. Still, this doesn't tell us why Tesave, of all parshiyot, was the one chosen.

The key may lie in Tesave's opening misva: to kindle "לְהַעֲלֹת נֵר תָּמִיד", the eternal flame of the Menorah. Rashi (on Shemot 27:20), based on Torat Kohanim, explains that "להעלות" means the Kohen must hold the flame to the wick until the fire rises on its own, burning independently. Just as the Torah is synonymous with Moshe, that is, he is the conduit through whom we received the teachings and misvot from G-d, so too his influence here endures even without his name. Like the "ner tamid," once the flame is passed on, it burns on its own. We know it's Moshe, even when not specifically mentioned.

The same is true for each of us. A word of encouragement, a moment of teaching, an act of kindness done for the sake of Heaven can become an eternal flame in someone else's life. A light that continues to burn, quietly and steadily, long after the moment has passed.
