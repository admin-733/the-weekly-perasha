In this week's perasha, Terumah, G-d instructs Moshe, "They shall make for Me a sanctuary" (Shemot 25:8). G-d uses the word "mikdash," translated by most as sanctuary. Rashi explains that "mikdash" means making for G-d's Name a "house of holiness," shifting the emphasis from sanctuary to house. Why does Rashi emphasize "house" instead of "sanctuary"? What is the difference between the two?

The Torah hints at the answer a few pesukim earlier: "From every man whose heart is so moved" (Shemot 25:2). G-d doesn't just ask for physical materials. He asks for a motivated heart first. The structure alone doesn't make a mikdash. It's the heart behind it that does.

I once came across a beautiful remez (allusion) that captures this perfectly. The gematria of "mikdash" is 444, "bayit" is 412, and the difference is 32, which equals the gematria of "lev," heart. The extra ingredient that turns a simple house into a sanctuary is the heart.

The second half of the pasuk supports this: "I will dwell among them" (Shemot 25:8), instead of "in it." The Ramban, Alshich, and others note that the Shechinah rests within the Jewish people themselves, not only in the physical building. When a Jew opens his heart and treats his home like a sanctuary, G-d states: "There, among them, I will dwell."

The Gemara (Megillah 29a) teaches that wherever the Jewish People were exiled, the Shechinah accompanied them. Even without the physical mikdash, G-d's presence traveled with His people. G-d's address is in our homes and in our hearts.

So how do we invite G-d into our homes? The good news is it doesn't have to be perfect. The Gemara (Pesahim 50b) teaches that even if you perform misvot not yet for the right reasons, you will eventually come to do them for the right reasons. In other words, even if you're not doing it purely for G-d right now, that's okay. Start anyway. Put up a mezuzah, say blessings out loud, keep Shabbat, speak kindly. Start with the actions and the heart follows. The atmosphere of your home is transformed, and G-d's presence follows.

In Da'at Tevunot, the Ramchal explains that misvot are pathways that draw us closer to G-d, perfecting the world and revealing His sovereignty, allowing us to cling to His presence.

Moreover, right after the Ten Commandments, G-d states: "In every place where I cause My Name to be mentioned, I will come to you and bless you" (Shemot 20:21). When we bring G-d's Name into our homes, He responds by bringing His blessing into that space.

Your home can be that place. Not just a house where you sleep and eat, but a space where G-d's presence is felt and His blessing flows in. That's the mikdash G-d is asking us to build. It starts right where you are, because home is where the heart is.
