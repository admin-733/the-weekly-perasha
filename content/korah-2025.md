In this week's perasha, we witness the dramatic rebellion of Qorah against Moshe & Aharon. Qorah, a prominent member of the tribe of Levi, challenges the appointment of Aharon as the Kohen HaGadol. Joining Qorah are Datan and Abiram and 250 men primarily from the tribe of Reuben. The standoff ends with the 250 men being consumed by a heavenly fire and Qorah and his followers being swallowed up by the Earth.

The Midrash Tanhuma asks a question regarding Qorah's motives, stating that Qorah was a clever man. What did he see that brought him to such a foolish undertaking?

The Midrash answers that his eyes misled him: Qorah saw that one of his descendants was the great Shemuel HaNabi. Qorah felt that Shemuel HaNabi stood equally in greatness to Moshe and Aharon as the pasuk in Tehillim states: "Moshe and Aharon were among His priests, and Shemuel was among those who invoke His name" (Tehillim 99:6). He saw that Shemuel was able to stand in the pasuk on his own with Moshe & Aharon and therefore felt his agenda held merit.

The story of Qorah's rebellion serves as a cautionary tale, reminding us of the dangers of pride, jealousy. The Pele Yoes, a classic work of Jewish ethics by Rabbi Eliezer Papo warns that jealousy can consume an entire person's life, leading them astray from the path of righteousness.

Now that we understand Qorah's motives, what about the 250 men from the Reuben? How did they get entangled in Qorah's rebellion?

The answer lies in the arrangement of the Israelite camp. The tribes of Reuben and Levi were neighbors in their encampment. By allowing themselves to be influenced by their neighbors and surrounding themselves with their presence, these 250 men were easily swayed to believe that they had a rightful claim to the firstborn status, instead of the tribe of Yehuda.

This teaches us a crucial lesson about the power of our surroundings and the influences we allow into our lives. We must be vigilant in guarding ourselves against negative influences, even when they come under the guise of friendship or good intentions. Once we open ourselves up to these influences, we become vulnerable to ideas and actions that we would never have considered if we were surrounded by righteous and just people.

As we study this perasha, let us take to heart the lessons of Qorah's downfall and the importance of choosing our influences wisely. By cultivating a spirit of humility, respect for Divine authority, and surrounding ourselves with positive role models, we can avoid the pitfalls of jealousy and pride, and merit to be true servants of G-D contributing to the harmony and spiritual growth of our community.
