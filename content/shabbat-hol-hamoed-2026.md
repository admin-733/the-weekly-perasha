As we enter Shabbat Hol HaMoed, we look back at just a few days ago, to our Sedarim. Before the sedarim, a few days before Pesah began, you gave it everything you had. The cleaning, the cooking, the checking every corner for hames, making sure the Seder plate was set and the guests knew what time to arrive. You probably told them an hour early just in case. You prepared your Divrei Torah, both old and new, and reviewed the halachot of Pesah.

When Pesah finally arrived and you sat down with your family for two amazing nights, hopefully they delivered. Two nights of real connection, real meaning, real family. Even if it did not go exactly as planned, you made it. You showed up. You landed.

Now that Hol HaMoed is here, it can feel like the rest of Pesah is just the aftermath. Even though we still recite the Hallel, count the Omer, read from the Torah and pray Musaf each day, these days carry their own kedushah and deserve our full respect. Still, our Sedarim gave us something very real and inspiring. The question is what we do with it now?

The word Seder means order and Rabbi Frand (Perashat Bo) teaches us that we are expected to carry what we felt at the Seder forward. The emunah we drew from the Seder is meant to carry us through the entire year.

The connection, the story, the inspiration, the sense that something real happened those two nights. That feeling was not meant to last just two nights. When something truly moves you, when someone leaves a real impression on you, you don't just let it fade. You hold onto it. You bring it back with you. We did not stay up late and tell the story of the Exodus just to close the Haggadah and move on.

Pesah is the first half of a process that concludes at Shabuot when we receive the Torah. The Ramban teaches (Vayikra 23:15) that the Omer we count each night is not just a countdown, it is a bridge from freedom to purpose. From leaving Egypt to standing at Sinai. The work does not end when the Seder plate is put away.

So for the rest of Pesah and beyond, ask yourself what you are carrying with you from those two nights. The feeling was real. The story is yours. Take it with you.
